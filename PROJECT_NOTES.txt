SMUDGes x AION-1: project notes
Written 2026-10-11. Numbers below come from the runs in this folder
(single train/test split, seed 42, one run each -- no error bars yet).

================================================================
1. WHAT WE ARE TRYING TO DO
================================================================
Question: how well do frozen AION-1 embeddings of Legacy Survey cutouts
recover the structural parameters of ultra-diffuse galaxy (UDG) candidates
from the SMUDGes catalog (Zaritsky+ 2023, ApJS 267, 27; catalog_folder/)?
This repeats the earlier LSBG (DES) benchmark experiments (exp1 regression,
exp3 tokenizer reconstruction) on a new dataset where the reference truth is
the catalog table2.dat. (exp2, morphology classification, is skipped: the
catalog has no morphology labels.)

Truth values: Re (arcsec), Sersic n, b/a and central surface brightness
mu0g, all BIAS-CORRECTED as value - bias (ReadMe convention). Dropped: VC != 0
(visually rejected), missing (-99.99) values/biases, non-positive corrected
Re/n/b-a. The catalog's own median 1-sigma is reported next to our RMSE as a
reference floor.

The size problem: AION's image tokenizer works on 96x96 images, but SMUDGes
galaxies have Re of ~5-20 arcsec (0.262 arcsec/px native), i.e. 20-80 native
pixels. A native 96x96 crop is only 25 arcsec across (~4 Re at Re=6").
Two options are being compared:
  - binned  : central 480x480 crop, 5x5 bins (1.31 arcsec/px) -> 96x96,
              126 arcsec across (>= 6 Re for Re <= ~21")
  - unbinned: central 96x96 native pixels (25 arcsec across)
  - intermediate factors 2 (192 crop, 0.52"/px, 50" wide) and 4 (384 crop,
    1.05"/px, 100" wide) to see where resolution and field of view trade off.
Bins are SUMMED (sum was found to work better than mean; sum conserves total
flux).  Heads: linear and 2-layer MLP (256 hidden); 256-MLP is the preferred
head, though in practice it is ~equal to linear here.

================================================================
2. DATA / PIPELINE
================================================================
download_smudges_cutouts.py : Legacy Survey cutout service, layer ls-dr10,
    512x512 px, 0.262"/px, bands griz, at each table2.dat RA/Dec. Resumable,
    HDF5 (smudges_ls_dr10_512.h5). 2600 of 7070 sources downloaded so far.
aion_smudges_common.py   : table2 reader + bias correction; H5 -> crop -> bin
    (cached .npz next to the H5); missing-band check; embedding extraction
    (cached); heads/metrics/heatmaps (copied from aion_lsbg_common.py).
exp1_structural_regression_smudges.py     : regression from embeddings
exp3_reconstruction_stress_test_smudges.py: tokenizer encode/decode MSE inside
    a 2*Re disc (aperture scales with Re in image pixels)
viz_binning_examples_smudges.py           : raw vs binned cutout pictures
Flags: --bin-factor {1,2,3,4,5}  --max-reff <arcsec> (default 6.0)
       exp3 only: --aperture-re (default 2)  --max-aperture-arcsec (default none)
       --bin-mode {sum,mean}     (default sum in exp1 and exp3)
Missing-band filter: a source is dropped if ANY band has >1% exactly-zero /
non-finite pixels in the central 480x480 (always that same region, so every
bin factor sees the identical sample). 413 of 1888 matched sources are dropped
with Re_corr <= 10"; the bulk are fully empty i-band (DR10 has no i coverage
over much of the field).

Sanity checks done (sample Re_corr <= 10", 1475 sources, all four factors):
  - all pixel values finite; exact-zero fraction ~0; names unique
  - stacked g+r+z image of the sample peaks at (47,47), flux-weighted
    centroid within 0.15-0.3 px of the array centre (47.5): centred at all
    factors (the small offset is the same at every factor; the stack of a
    noisy sample is not precise below that)
  - sum-binned cubes match a manual 5x5 sum of the raw H5 crop to 1e-5, and
    total flux per band is conserved
  - crops are symmetric about the cutout centre (offsets 16/48/144/192 px for
    factors 5/4/2/1), and 96 is even so bins are symmetric about the centre
  - NOT cleaned: bright foreground stars / neighbours are left in. Summed
    pixel maxima reach ~1500 (x5) against a 99th percentile of ~1, i.e. a
    few cutouts contain very bright objects. AION is trained on such fields
    but they may affect individual objects.

3. RESULTS SO FAR
================================================================
(a) Re_corr <= 6", binned (x5) vs unbinned (x1), 392 sources, 118 test:
                  RMSE binned/unbinned      R2 binned/unbinned (MLP-256)
    Re              0.206 / 0.193             0.01 / 0.14
    n               0.206 / 0.154            -0.02 / 0.43
    mu0g            0.401 / 0.251             0.52 / 0.81
    b/a             0.110 / 0.116             0.51 / 0.46
  Re is uninformative in that sample: Re_corr only spans ~5.3-6", so the
  spread is smaller than the catalog 1-sigma (0.40") and R2 ~ 0.

(b) Re_corr <= 10", 1475 sources (1032 train / 443 test), MLP-256,
    sum binning.  Test-set spread of truth: Re 1.26", n 0.27, mu0g 0.68,
    b/a 0.16.  RMSE (R2) by bin factor:
                  x1             x2             x4             x5         catalog 1-sigma
    Re (arcsec)   0.891 (0.50)   0.821 (0.58)   0.809 (0.59)   0.869 (0.53)   0.615
    n             0.180 (0.54)   0.176 (0.56)   0.203 (0.42)   0.235 (0.22)   0.105
    mu0g (mag)    0.268 (0.85)   0.262 (0.85)   0.345 (0.75)   0.411 (0.64)   0.180
    b/a           0.114 (0.48)   0.082 (0.73)   0.092 (0.67)   0.103 (0.58)   0.050
  Same runs, MAE / MSE (MSE = RMSE^2) / R2 for MLP-256:
                x1               x2               x4               x5
    Re    MAE .715 MSE .794  .639 .675  .649 .654  .685 .755
    n     MAE .137 MSE .032  .131 .031  .154 .041  .178 .055
    mu0g  MAE .202 MSE .072  .199 .069  .261 .119  .311 .169
    b/a   MAE .091 MSE .013  .064 .007  .072 .008  .081 .011
  (R2 as in the table above; linear-head values are in metrics.csv.)
  Linear head gives nearly identical numbers (differences ~0.01-0.03 in RMSE,
  slightly worse than the MLP in most cells).

(c) exp3, same sample, tokenizer reconstruction MSE inside 2*Re (fractional:
    error power / image power in the aperture), median over 1475 sources:
        x1 0.556   x2 0.383   x4 0.414   x5 0.425
    The error grows with mu0g: the faintest tertile (highest mu0g) is worst
    at every factor (x1: 0.36 -> 0.55 -> 0.76 from brightest to faintest).
    Errors of ~0.4-0.55 mean the reconstructions explain only about half the
    in-aperture image power, because faint-galaxy images are noise-dominated.

    More exp3 detail (same 1475 sources, mean / median / p90 of recon MSE):
        x1 .533/.556/.871   x2 .415/.383/.731   x4 .433/.414/.762   x5 .450/.425/.798
    Paired: x2 beats x1 for 84% of sources (mean diff 0.118 +- 0.005);
    x2 vs x4 / x5 differ by only 0.02-0.04 (x2 better ~55% / 52% of sources).
    ADOPTED SETTING: aperture = disc of radius 2*Re (as in the original LSBG
    exp3), limited only by the image frame (48 px).  x2, x4, x5 all see the
    same 2*Re patch of sky (median ~570 arcsec^2 at each).  Only x1 is limited
    by its frame (12.4" radius): median 2*Re is 13.5", so the aperture is
    clipped for 66% of sources, losing a median 15% of the area (mean 21%,
    90th percentile 53%).  (The "median aperture 7211 px^2" figure is an AREA
    in px^2 -- about 495 arcsec^2, the whole 96x96 frame's inscribed disc.)
    This does not drive the x1 deficit: the x1 - x2 MSE gap is 0.18 for the
    499 sources whose 2*Re fits in the frame and 0.13 for the 976 clipped
    ones, i.e. LARGER when unclipped.

(d) exp3 SENSITIVITY CHECK, not the adopted setting: aperture = 1.5*Re_corr
    capped at 12" at every factor (25.2% of sources clipped; median radius
    10.1"), so all factors, x1 included, see an identical sky region.  Same
    1475 sources.  Recon MSE:
                       x1     x2     x4     x5
        mean          .523   .370   .324   .329
        median        .530   .326   .279   .279
        p90           .863   .694   .627   .633
        median aperture area (img px^2)  4709  1185  293  185
    Paired: x2 beats x1 for 90% of sources (+0.152 +- 0.004); x4 beats x2 for
    69% (x2 - x4 = +0.047 +- 0.005); x4 vs x5 indistinguishable (-0.006 +-
    0.002).  The ordering holds in every Re, mu0g and n tertile, and for
    clipped (Re > 8") and unclipped sources alike.  Error again grows with
    mu0g (faintest tertile: x1 .77, x2 .55, x4 .44, x5 .42).
    x1 is still clearly the worst, confirming the x1 clamp is not the cause.
    BUT the ranking among the binned factors flipped relative to 2*Re:
    at 2*Re x2 was best (median x2 .383, x4 .414, x5 .425) while with this
    smaller, capped aperture x4 ~ x5 (.279) beat x2 (.326).  So exp3's
    ordering of x2/x4/x5 depends on the aperture; only "x1 is worst" is
    robust.  (Cause not tested.  A sweep over 1, 1.5, 2 Re with and without
    a cap would map it.)  The metric is error power / image power
    and is noise-dominated for faint galaxies, so a lot of this is the
    higher per-pixel S/N of binned images, not tokenizer quality as such.

================================================================
4. WHAT THIS SUGGESTS (tentative)
================================================================
 - Heavy binning (x5) hurts n and mu0g clearly (n R2 0.54 -> 0.22, mu0g RMSE
   0.27 -> 0.41 going x1 -> x5). Heavy binning blurs the centre/profile shape.
 - x1 and x2 are statistically close for n and mu0g; x2 is best or tied for
   b/a, n and mu0g (exp1); x4 is marginally best for Re. For exp1 the best
   compromise between resolution and field of view looks like factor 2
   (0.52"/px, 50" wide). Re prefers wider field of view (x2/x4 over x1) as expected,
   but the Re differences (0.81-0.89") are small relative to the sampling
   noise at N=443, so not firm.
 - Re is recovered with RMSE ~0.8-0.9" (R2 ~0.5-0.6); this is above the
   catalog 1-sigma (0.6"), so there is room, but the catalog truth itself is
   noisy at this level.
 - exp1 and exp3 disagree on x1: exp3 (2 Re aperture) has x1 << x2 < x4 <
   x5, while exp1's structural accuracy is best at x1/x2 and degrades at
   x4/x5.  Unbinned images are noisier per pixel, so the tokenizer
   reconstructs them worse, even though the embedding retains more
   structural information.  x2 is best on exp3 among binned factors at the
   adopted 2*Re aperture and best/tied in exp1; but see (d): the exp3
   ordering of x2/x4/x5 is aperture dependent.
 - Earlier worry that binning inflates arcsec-scale errors by ~5x (pixel-scale
   floor) was not borne out for Re once the sample has a wide Re range.

================================================================
5. CAVEATS / OPEN ITEMS
================================================================
 - One 70/30 split, one seed. Early stopping uses the TEST set as validation
   (inherited from exp1 of the LSBG suite) so scores are slightly optimistic.
   Needs: several seeds or CV for error bars; a separate validation split.
 - Only 2600 / 7070 sources downloaded; i-band missing for ~20% of those.
 - Selection: catalog Re >= 5.3" and cuts at <=6"/<=10" make the Re range
   narrow; R2 for Re is therefore depressed relative to a full-range sample.
 - Mean pooling over tokens in the embedding discards position information.
 - Bright stars/neighbours not masked.
 - Cutouts are DR10 (ls-dr10); the catalog was measured on DR9 images.
 - Untested: factor 3, larger Re (>10") where factor 5's wide field should
   matter, sum vs mean re-check at x2, fine-tuning instead of frozen heads.
